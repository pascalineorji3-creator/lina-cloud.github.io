Project 06 · Data Analysis

Digital Access Across African Countries

What does public data tell us about internet use across six African countries? This exploratory analysis compares reported internet use, highlights differences, and considers what the figures can and cannot tell us.

Exploratory analysis World Bank open data Data interpretation 2023 comparison

01 / The question

Internet connectivity can affect access to education, digital services, information, and economic opportunities. This project examines reported internet use in Nigeria, Ghana, Kenya, South Africa, Rwanda, and Senegal.

The primary question is: how different were reported internet use rates across these six countries in 2023?

This is a descriptive comparison. It does not attempt to prove why the countries differ or rank their overall digital development.

04 / Key findings

South Africa recorded the highest rate

South Africa's reported rate was approximately 78.3%, the highest among the six countries in this comparison.

Internet use varied substantially

The difference between South Africa (78.3%) and Rwanda (28.8%) was about 49.5 percentage points. This describes the range across this selected group, not the whole continent.

Nigeria was below three countries in this group

Nigeria's reported rate was approximately 40.1%, below South Africa, Ghana, and Senegal, but above Kenya and Rwanda.

Interpretation: the figures show differences in reported internet use. They do not by themselves explain the causes of those differences.

05 / Method

  1. Define the measure. Use the World Bank indicator “Individuals using the Internet (% of population).”
  2. Select the comparison group. Retrieve reported values for six named countries for 2023.
  3. Compare like with like. Use the same indicator and year for every country.
  4. Calculate the range. Subtract the lowest reported rate from the highest.
  5. Communicate carefully. Round percentages to one decimal place and avoid treating association or difference as proof of cause.

06 / Limitations

  • The comparison covers six selected countries, not all African countries.
  • The indicator measures reported internet use. It does not directly measure connection quality, affordability, speed, or frequency of use.
  • International datasets may be revised, and reporting methods or data availability may differ.
  • This is a descriptive comparison. Additional data and methods would be needed to investigate causes or outcomes.

What this project demonstrates

This work sample demonstrates question framing, use of a public data source, comparison of quantitative observations, clear visual communication, and attention to analytical limitations.

The next step for a deeper analysis would be to examine country-level trends over time, investigate data consistency, and combine internet-use figures with additional indicators such as affordability or network coverage.
