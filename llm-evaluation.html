LLM EVALUATION · CASE STUDY 01

Comparative Evaluation of LLMs for Operational Data Analysis

A controlled evaluation of two large language models using the same dataset, identical instructions, a fixed scoring rubric, and independent numerical verification.

Evaluation Objective

The objective was to examine how consistently two LLMs could analyze the same operational dataset while following strict instructions not to invent information.

The evaluation focused not only on numerical correctness, but also on analytical depth, data-quality awareness, business usefulness, and transparency.

Dataset note: This is a synthetic portfolio demonstration dataset. It does not represent a real company, client, or confidential business data.

Dataset & Task

150 Operational records
6 Departments
8 Categories
5 Communication channels
3 Operational statuses
2 Models evaluated

Evaluation Method

  1. The same CSV dataset was supplied to both models.
  2. The same analysis prompt and requirements were supplied to both models.
  3. Outputs were examined for factual accuracy, instruction following, analytical depth, data-quality awareness, business usefulness, and transparency.
  4. Numerical claims were cross-checked against the source dataset.
  5. Each criterion was scored on a 1–5 scale.

Evaluation Rubric

Factual Accuracy

Did the model report numbers and observations supported by the source dataset?

Instruction Following

Did the model follow the requested structure, constraints, and requirement not to invent information?

Analytical Depth

Did the model identify meaningful patterns beyond basic counts?

Data-Quality Awareness

Did the model identify limitations, anomalies, or structural issues in the dataset?

Business Usefulness

Were the insights and recommendations practical and grounded in the available evidence?

Transparency

Did the model clearly distinguish observed facts, interpretation, recommendations, and limitations?

Observed Dataset Findings

Operational Status

98 records were resolved (65.3%), 22 were pending (14.7%), and 30 were escalated (20.0%).

Priority Distribution

Medium priority was the most frequent category with 78 records, followed by Low (32), High (31), and Critical (9).

Resolution Time

Average resolution time among resolved records was 3.49 hours, with a median of 2.8 hours.

Satisfaction

The available satisfaction scores averaged 4.23/5 across 98 scored records.

Key Evaluation Observations

ChatGPT

ChatGPT correctly reported the major numerical findings and followed the requested analysis structure. It also identified important limitations surrounding the satisfaction data.

The evaluation identified opportunities for deeper data-quality inspection and additional segmentation.

Claude

Claude correctly reported the major findings and provided additional analytical detail, including category escalation patterns and deeper resolution-time breakdowns.

It also identified that Category and Request_Type were one-to-one mappings and flagged ambiguity surrounding Resolution_Hours on escalated records.

Important Limitations

  • The dataset is synthetic and therefore does not represent real organizational performance.
  • Satisfaction scores were available only for resolved records, limiting what can be concluded about pending and escalated cases.
  • The meaning of Resolution_Hours for escalated records was not sufficiently defined for treating those values as standard resolution times.
  • The evaluation compares two model outputs from one controlled task; results should not be generalized to all tasks or models.
  • Model behavior can vary with prompts, datasets, versions, and task design.
